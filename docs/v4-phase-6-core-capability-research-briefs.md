# Anti-Gravity OS v4 — Core Capability Research Briefs

**Status:** research gate before further Phase 6 route or skill redesign  
**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**How to use:** Run each brief independently in a deep-research tool. Do not combine them into one oversized request. Return the full source-backed report with working links.

## Shared research standard

Apply these rules to every brief:

1. Research the decision, not a fashionable framework. Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed work, major practitioner publications with transparent evidence, and official technical documentation where relevant.
2. State each source's publication date and the research search date. Flag unsupported claims, weak evidence, and disagreement.
3. Separate durable principles from organisation-specific process, stack/tool-specific advice, and current-but-volatile claims.
4. Do not assume a large company, a product-management department, a fixed software stack, RAG, autonomous agents, mandatory analytics, or a rigid waterfall.
5. Treat this as a personal AI Product Studio: one human goal owner, capable AI, optional on-demand specialist agents, and projects ranging from software products to websites, internal tools, and client work.
6. The goal is not to teach basic theory to the model. Identify compact guidance, decision checks, examples, references, and evaluation cases that make an AI agent more reliable than an unaided model.
7. For every proposed OS addition, specify its correct home: always-on policy, skill command file, selectively loaded reference, workflow route, tool adapter, project context, evaluation fixture, or nowhere.
8. Include a section called **What not to encode**. Explain what should remain model judgement, project-specific context, or a user decision.

## Brief 1 — Product Thinking

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study to improve the Product Thinking capability of a personal AI Product Studio.

Context
The Studio helps one human direct AI to build useful digital products, websites, internal tools, client work, and occasionally AI-enabled features. It must help decide what to build and why, but must not turn every small approved change into a formal product-management ceremony. The intended result is a compact AI skill plus selectively loaded references, not a textbook or a company playbook.

Research question
What repeatable product decisions and evidence practices should an AI agent use to improve the quality of product framing, prioritisation, MVP scope, assumptions, and outcome learning — beyond what a strong general model already knows?

Investigate and compare, only where evidence supports it:
1. Problem discovery and problem framing: user jobs, unmet needs, constraints, alternatives, and value hypotheses.
2. Evidence quality: how to combine user reports, behavioural evidence, qualitative research, domain knowledge, and uncertainty without dismissing any one source.
3. Opportunity and prioritisation decisions: expected value, reach/impact/effort, opportunity cost, risks, reversibility, and when simple prioritisation frameworks are misleading.
4. MVPs, experiments, prototypes, pilots, and staged delivery: when each is appropriate; how to define a learning goal without reducing quality.
5. Outcome measurement: when instrumentation is worthwhile, when it is not, and credible low-cost alternatives for small, private, early-stage, or client projects.
6. Product strategy for existing products versus greenfield ideas; consumer, B2B, internal-tool, service, and website cases.
7. AI-enabled product features: the additional product questions that matter before deciding to use an AI feature, without assuming retrieval, agents, memory, or automation.

Required analysis
- Distinguish durable principles from named-framework vocabulary that should not become mandatory OS language.
- Challenge overconfident claims such as "users always lie", "all features require analytics", or "every task needs a discovery phase".
- Compare a lightweight decision approach, a normal product-discovery approach, and a high-stakes/irreversible approach. State clear escalation triggers.
- Identify the smallest decision record an AI should return for a small change, an MVP, and a high-stakes product bet.

Deliverable format
1. Executive answer: the 8–12 highest-confidence findings.
2. Evidence table: claim, source, evidence strength, scope/limits, and whether it is durable.
3. Decision map: task signal → questions to ask → evidence needed → suitable action.
4. Candidate Product Thinking skill contents: activation, exclusions, core reasoning rules, output shape, and selective-reference topics.
5. Candidate Project Inception workflow implications, but do not design the workflow itself.
6. What not to encode in the OS.
7. Five positive and five negative evaluation scenarios that could test whether a revised skill improves AI behaviour.
8. Open disagreements, uncertainties, and research gaps.

Use direct source links. Do not provide generic product-management advice without showing why it applies to this Studio.
```

## Brief 2 — Project Inception and AI-Assisted Delivery Shaping

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study on how a personal AI Product Studio should take a vague idea or incoming project from first conversation to a decision-ready first direction.

Context
The Studio is not a traditional company workflow. One human remains goal owner. A Studio Director can activate Product, Architecture, Design, Engineering, and Assurance help only when needed. The Studio supports new digital products, existing-product improvements, client websites, internal tools, and AI-enabled features. It must avoid both two failure modes: building immediately with no shared understanding, and forcing every idea through a long rigid waterfall.

Research question
What are the minimum durable decisions, artefacts, gates, and handoffs that materially improve early-stage project success for AI-assisted work? Which are conditional, and which should never be imposed by default?

Investigate
1. Starting points: greenfield idea, existing product improvement, narrowly scoped request, client brief, technical rescue, and regulated/high-impact work.
2. The relationship between product framing, user journey/flow, design exploration, architecture, delivery planning, risk reduction, and verification planning.
3. How experienced teams avoid premature technology choices, premature detail, scope explosion, and fake certainty.
4. How discovery and delivery can loop rather than act as one sequential handoff.
5. Decision records, assumptions, unknowns, risks, non-goals, acceptance signals, and reversibility.
6. The smallest useful project truth that an AI agent should persist in a workspace, and the privacy/authority boundaries for that information.
7. The special case of user-facing AI features: suitability, quality bar, failure tolerance, grounding/data access, evaluation, cost, and human control.
8. How an orchestrating agent should decide whether to work directly, call a functional lead, or create a bounded worker — without creating a permanent swarm.

Required analysis
- Define three proportional paths: small reversible change, normal product/MVP, and high-impact or irreversible initiative.
- For each proposed gate, state the risk it prevents, the evidence it requires, and the conditions under which it should be skipped.
- Explicitly reject artefacts/processes that look professional but add no decision value for a small studio.
- Distinguish stable cross-project guidance from project-specific context templates and task-scoped state.

Deliverable format
1. Executive answer: minimum viable inception model and its limits.
2. Decision-to-artefact table: decision, owner, evidence, artefact (if any), escalation trigger, and non-use condition.
3. Non-waterfall collaboration model for Product, Architecture, Design, Engineering, and Assurance.
4. Candidate `project-inception` workflow states, inputs, outputs, stop conditions, and next-route rules.
5. Candidate Studio Director routing rules for direct work versus functional lead versus bounded worker.
6. What not to encode in the workflow or global router.
7. Five realistic task simulations, including one tiny local change and one high-impact AI feature.
8. Open disagreements, uncertainties, and research gaps.

Use direct source links. Do not assume every project needs formal research, a full UI design stage, a fixed tech stack, or deployment planning.
```

## Brief 3 — AI-Assisted Debugging

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study to design a high-reliability debugging capability for a personal AI Product Studio.

Context
The goal is not a generic debugging checklist. The capability should help an AI agent diagnose real software failures more reliably than a strong raw model: it must reduce premature guesses, reproduce failures where possible, inspect evidence, reason about state and boundaries, choose targeted experiments, implement the smallest justified fix when authorised, and verify that the failure is actually resolved.

Research question
Which debugging methods, evidence practices, and AI-agent controls measurably improve fault diagnosis and repair across application code, APIs, databases, integrations, build systems, UI, performance, and production-like incidents?

Investigate
1. Scientific debugging: symptom definition, observation, hypothesis generation, falsification, minimal reproduction, experiment design, and stopping rules.
2. Fault-localisation methods: stack traces, logs, tracing, metrics, differential diagnosis, bisection, dependency/commit/configuration comparison, and state-transition analysis.
3. Boundary-focused debugging: inputs, outputs, contracts, persistence, authorization, concurrency, timing, caching, environment/configuration, third-party dependencies, and UI state.
4. Debugging under uncertainty: how to label a hypothesis, avoid changing code before a mechanism is established, and decide when an incident needs mitigation before root cause is known.
5. Repair discipline: smallest safe fix, regression protection, checking genuinely similar sibling paths, rollback/reversibility, and verifying in an appropriate environment.
6. AI-assisted software-engineering research: failure modes of code agents during diagnosis, tool-use evidence, hallucinated root causes, shallow test fixes, and methods that improve reliability.
7. Different risk levels: local bug, flaky test, data-integrity issue, security concern, and production incident.

Required analysis
- Separate durable debugging logic from stack-specific commands and observability tools.
- Define when a diagnosis must remain read-only and when an implementation may begin.
- Identify which detailed decision trees belong in references rather than the main skill.
- Explain what an AI must return to distinguish confirmed root cause, likely cause, ruled-out cause, and unverified theory.
- Do not claim an AI can guarantee fault detection or that every bug needs the same process.

Deliverable format
1. Executive answer: the 8–12 highest-confidence mechanisms for reliable AI debugging.
2. Evidence table: claim, source, strength, limits, and OS home.
3. Candidate debugging skill: activation, exclusions, core loop, evidence standards, repair/approval boundary, output shape, and reference-loading rules.
4. Candidate `debug-issue` workflow modes: diagnose, propose, implement, and incident-mitigate.
5. A compact fault-class map with appropriate first checks and false-positive risks.
6. What not to encode.
7. A behaviour evaluation plan: at least six fixtures, including a misleading stack trace, configuration fault, state/cache fault, concurrency/timing fault, third-party failure, and a case where no code change is justified.
8. Open disagreements, uncertainties, and research gaps.

Use direct source links and distinguish empirical findings from practitioner heuristics.
```

## Brief 4 — Testing and Verification for AI-Assisted Engineering

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study to improve Testing and Verification capabilities for a personal AI Product Studio.

Context
The Studio needs AI agents that do not confuse a passing unit test, static scan, or coverage percentage with genuine confidence. They must choose evidence proportionate to the user risk and change surface, write maintainable tests, protect real boundaries, expose false confidence, and state what remains unverified. The intended output is a portable testing skill, a focused test-strategy route, and a verification route — not a mandate for any framework, cloud service, database, or fixed test pyramid.

Research question
What evidence-selection, test-design, and verification practices should an AI agent use to increase confidence in AI-assisted software changes while controlling test-maintenance cost and avoiding false proof?

Investigate
1. Risk-based test strategy: behaviour criticality, change surface, reversibility, data/security consequences, and confidence versus maintenance cost.
2. The appropriate roles and limits of static analysis, types, unit tests, integration tests, contract tests, property/metamorphic tests, fuzzing, browser tests, end-to-end tests, manual/exploratory checks, canaries, and production monitoring.
3. Boundary realism: contracts, persistence, authorization, queues, caches, configuration, network dependencies, time, concurrency, migrations, and UI journeys.
4. Good regression tests after a real bug: how to protect the mechanism rather than encode an implementation accident.
5. Flakiness, test isolation, determinism, fixtures, mocks/fakes/stubs, and external dependency control.
6. Testing AI-written changes and AI-feature products: evaluating model/instruction changes, nondeterminism, groundedness, tool boundaries, cost/latency, safety, and human review where relevant.
7. Release verification: why baseline scanners are useful but insufficient, and what evidence is needed before a higher-impact release.

Required analysis
- Compare test-pyramid, trophy, and risk-based approaches; state where each helps or fails. Do not prescribe one universal shape.
- Separate durable testing principles from framework/tool instructions that belong in project context or a tool adapter.
- Define when an AI should say "unverified" rather than infer success.
- Distinguish the responsibilities of a Testing skill, `test-strategy` workflow, `verify-project` workflow, and security review.

Deliverable format
1. Executive answer: the 8–12 highest-confidence findings.
2. Evidence table: claim, source, strength, limits, and OS home.
3. Candidate testing skill: activation, exclusions, decision matrix, output shape, reference-loading rules, and anti-patterns.
4. Candidate `test-strategy` and `verify-project` workflow boundaries and handoffs.
5. Evidence matrix for five change classes: small local fix, API/data contract change, authentication/authorization change, database migration, and user-facing AI feature.
6. What not to encode.
7. A behaviour evaluation plan with positive and negative cases showing whether the revised guidance improves an AI agent over raw model output.
8. Open disagreements, uncertainties, and research gaps.

Use direct source links. Do not equate a green scanner, high coverage, or a passing mocked test with release readiness.
```
