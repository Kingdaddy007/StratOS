# Deep Research Brief — Colour Systems for an AI Product Studio

## Copy this prompt into a deep-research tool

```text
Conduct a rigorous, decision-focused, source-backed research study for a personal AI Product Studio that designs software products, websites, brand surfaces, and optional cinematic/spatial experiences.

This is not a request for a generic colour-theory article or a palette generator. The result will decide how to replace or demote an existing AI-agent skill called `color-system`.

The current skill is unsafe as an authority because it makes broad claims such as: fixed emotional meanings for hues; gender/age colour preferences; a red CTA outperforming green; colour harmony formulas as general rules; and claims about psychology without clear current evidence. Do not accept those claims without strong evidence.

## Primary decision

What is the smallest durable method an AI agent needs to make better colour decisions without becoming formulaic, culturally stereotyped, inaccessible, or overconfident?

## Context

The OS has:
- a Studio Director that routes work;
- a Design Director responsible for UI/UX and optional spatial/media design;
- a `ui-ux` skill that already covers user flow, states, responsive behaviour, accessibility, and visual hierarchy;
- an optional Spatial pack for interiors, architecture, showrooms, and cinematic websites;
- a requirement that specialist instructions must help a capable AI model rather than box it in.

We must decide whether colour deserves:
1. a standalone skill;
2. a selective reference inside UI/UX and Brand/Spatial work; or
3. only project-specific design tokens and accessibility checks.

## Research rules

1. Prefer primary standards, official specifications, peer-reviewed systematic reviews, and authoritative accessibility guidance.
2. Clearly separate established standards, well-supported findings, limited/context-dependent evidence, practitioner convention, and unsupported marketing claims.
3. Do not turn a study from one page, audience, culture, or experiment into a universal design rule.
4. Cite every material factual claim with direct links and publication/verification dates.
5. If sources disagree or evidence is weak, say so plainly. Do not fill gaps with confident advice.
6. Treat vendor blogs, popular design articles, and isolated A/B tests as weak evidence unless corroborated.
7. Do not write the final SKILL.md. Produce a decision report and a proposed compact method only.

## Questions to answer

### A. Durable core
What colour decisions are genuinely reusable across product UI, marketing/brand pages, and spatial/editorial experiences?

Assess at least:
- semantic roles and design tokens;
- text and non-text contrast, focus indicators, states, charts/data visualisation, and dark/light modes;
- hierarchy, grouping, status/feedback, error/success/warning meanings;
- responsive/device/environment variation;
- colour-vision deficiency and other accessibility limits;
- use of imagery, typography, material, and spacing when colour should *not* carry the identity alone.

### B. Claims that must be rejected or heavily qualified
Assess the evidence quality for:
- universal colour psychology;
- gender/age/cultural colour preference generalisations;
- “red versus green CTA” conversion claims;
- colour-wheel harmony as a decision engine;
- colour labels such as “blue equals trust” or “purple equals luxury.”

For each, state: supported, context-dependent, weak, or unsupported; explain what an AI should do instead.

### C. Context separation
How should the method differ—if at all—between:
1. product UI and transactional interfaces;
2. brand/marketing pages; and
3. cinematic/spatial/editorial experiences?

State which rules are universal and which must remain project-specific judgement.

### D. AI-agent operating method
Propose the smallest useful decision sequence an AI agent can follow before recommending or implementing colour:
- required inputs;
- when to request missing brand/project context;
- when to reuse existing tokens rather than invent new colour;
- what must be measured or checked;
- what requires visual review rather than a textual claim;
- how to state uncertainty;
- how to hand off an implementation-ready semantic token system.

Make it conditional and judgment-supporting, not a mandatory palette recipe.

### E. OS architecture decision
Recommend one of the following, with reasoning:
- keep a standalone `color-system` skill;
- merge a compact colour method into `ui-ux`, with detailed material as indexed references;
- make colour only a project-context/template concern.

Name the exact triggers, exclusions, inputs, outputs, verification checks, and reference categories for your recommendation.

### F. Audit of the current skill
Provide a concise table with columns:
`Current claim or pattern | Evidence assessment | Risk if kept | Replace with`

Include the current skill's CTA claim, colour psychology tables, demographic/cultural generalisations, harmony formulas, print/mobile requirements, and accessibility statements.

## Deliverable

Return a decision report with:

1. Executive recommendation.
2. Evidence-quality summary.
3. Universal core versus context-dependent judgement.
4. Smallest durable AI-agent method.
5. Proposed OS placement and routing.
6. Current-skill claim audit.
7. Source list with direct URLs and dates.
8. What remains uncertain and must be checked on a real project.

Do not recommend a fixed palette, a specific visual style, or a model/provider. Do not claim that colour alone determines trust, conversion, emotion, luxury, or user behaviour.
```
