# Focused research prompt — Ethical behavioural insight for a personal AI Product Studio

Copy the prompt below into a deep-research tool as a single message. It is a **research assignment**. Do not ask me to attach a returned report or a separate brief; conduct the research and return the deliverable described here.

---

## Assignment

Conduct a rigorous, source-backed decision study for a one-person, AI-assisted Product Studio. The Studio builds websites, digital products, services, client offers, and occasional cinematic/spatial experiences. It needs a small, safe capability for using behavioural insight to improve clarity, comprehension, trust, accessibility, and informed decision-making.

This assignment is **not** asking for a generic marketing playbook, a conversion-hack list, or a collection of famous biases. It must decide what belongs in an AI operating system without making a model imitate tactics it cannot substantiate or ethically apply.

## Existing asset to assess

The current `marketing-psychology` skill contains:

- general thinking models such as first principles, jobs to be done, inversion, constraints, opportunity cost, second-order thinking, and probabilistic thinking;
- buyer/behaviour labels such as familiarity, attribution, confirmation, social proof, scarcity, loss aversion, anchoring, framing, default effects, choice overload, goal progress, endowment, sunk cost, regret aversion, and pricing heuristics;
- tactics and examples including scarcity/urgency, “foot-in-the-door,” “door-in-the-face,” decoy pricing, pre-selected plans, free trials, retargeting, and creator/social proof;
- product/UX concepts such as cognitive load, choice architecture, onboarding, activation, and experiment ideas.

Some material is useful as a diagnostic lens; some belongs with Product Thinking, UI/UX, Page CRO, Testing, or Pricing; some may be context-dependent, ethically risky, poorly evidenced, or inappropriate for a portable default.

## Decision to make

Recommend the **smallest durable operating model** for this capability. Determine:

1. Whether it should remain a standalone skill, become a conditional shared reference, or be split across existing capabilities.
2. Which current concepts should be **preserved**, **preserved but conditional**, **moved/routed**, **demoted to a candidate reference**, or **rejected**.
3. The exact ethical boundary between helping a person understand and decide versus manipulating, coercing, obscuring, exploiting vulnerability, or using deceptive design.
4. How an AI should turn a behavioural idea into a testable, reversible, context-limited hypothesis instead of claiming it will increase conversion.
5. What evidence/provenance is required before recommending a public claim, a price/comparison, social proof, urgency, default, trial, or onboarding mechanism.
6. How this should work for a small studio with limited traffic and no licence to run uncontrolled public experiments, rather than assuming a large SaaS growth team.

## Required distinctions

Make these boundaries explicit:

- **General reasoning** versus behavioural insight: do not duplicate core reasoning models just because they have a marketing example.
- **Product value and product decisions** (`product-thinking`) versus communication (`copywriting`, `copy-editing`, `expert-positioning`).
- **Interface/task design and accessibility** (`ui-ux`) versus page/conversion diagnosis (`page-cro`) versus behavioural insight.
- **Experiment design and causal claims** (`testing`) versus a behavioural hypothesis.
- **Professional-services selling** (`expert-positioning`), prospect preparation (`prospect-research`), and sales collateral (`sales-enablement`) versus generic “persuasion.”
- **Spatial/cinematic storytelling** (a conditional specialist pack) versus portable behaviour rules.
- **Read-only research and local drafting** versus external contact, publication, data collection, advertising spend, pricing changes, A/B tests, or other external effects requiring explicit human approval.

## Evidence standards

- Prefer systematic reviews, meta-analyses, high-quality primary studies, official standards/regulators, and authoritative accessibility/privacy guidance.
- Use primary or official sources for current standards and guidance. Cite direct URLs and publication/update dates.
- Treat research from one sector, channel, country, population, or experiment as context-limited.
- Distinguish a measured association, an experimental effect, a plausible mechanism, practitioner convention, and a Studio design recommendation.
- Do not present a bias label as a guaranteed persuasion lever. Do not assume that a “statistically significant” effect is useful, ethical, or transferable.
- Explicitly identify findings that are contested, small, non-replicated, highly moderated, or not appropriate for a portable AI instruction.
- Do not give legal advice. Identify jurisdiction-specific issues for later review.

## Ethical floor

Reject or tightly constrain fake scarcity, fabricated/altered testimonials, deceptive comparisons, hidden defaults, confirmshaming, obstruction of cancellation, personal-data exploitation, stereotype-based targeting, manipulative exploitation of vulnerable people, false urgency, and presenting synthetic material as documentary evidence.

Treat authentic proof, transparent constraints, clear language, accessible choices, honest comparison, and user-beneficial friction reduction as potential areas of work—but only with the appropriate evidence and task context.

## Required deliverable

Return a concise but substantive report with these sections:

1. **Executive decision:** the recommended capability shape and why; name the option you reject and its cost.
2. **Evidence map:** what the best sources actually support, with direct links and limits.
3. **Current-asset ledger:** for every major group above, mark Preserve / Conditional / Move / Demote / Reject, explain why, and give the new owner or loading condition.
4. **Safe operating protocol:** activation, exclusions, smallest-task path, evidence/claim labels, ethical stops, handoffs, output shape, and approval boundaries.
5. **Hypothesis protocol:** how to frame, test, measure, and interpret a behavioural hypothesis without causal overclaim; include low-traffic alternatives.
6. **Portable scenarios:** one product page, one professional-service offer, one UI/onboarding task, one ordinary copy edit, one spatial/cinematic website, and one high-risk/regulated or vulnerable-audience case. For each: state what activates, what does not, and the stop condition.
7. **Disconfirmation:** what future evidence or Studio use would make the recommendation wrong.
8. **Implementation ledger:** no more than 12 concrete changes to the current skill/reference/workflow system, each labelled immediate / evidence-gated / reject.

Keep the report oriented to **decision quality**, not an encyclopedia of psychology. Do not assume the Studio needs a separate “growth department,” a universal funnel, a mandatory persona exercise, or a marketing tactic for every task.
