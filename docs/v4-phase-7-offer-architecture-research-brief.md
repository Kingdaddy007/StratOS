# Offer Architecture Research Gate

**Status:** required before creating `offer-architecture`  
**Scope:** one-person, AI-assisted Product Studio; products, services, websites, software, and optional high-trust creative work  
**Boundary:** this is a research request, not a prompt to write the skill or to recommend external action.

## Why this research is separate

The Growth department decision report established that offer architecture is a genuinely missing capability: it owns the recurring decision of what is offered, to whom, with which inclusions, exclusions, proof, risk boundaries, price hypothesis, and next step. That report does **not** establish the detailed method. Do not create a capability merely from a fluent summary; first gather and compare independent evidence.

Run the two briefs separately. Each report must stand on its own. Do not attach this file to a tool that treats it as a missing source document; paste the complete selected brief directly into the research tool.

---

## Brief A — Offer Design, Scope, Value, and Price Hypotheses

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study to define the smallest durable operating method for OFFER ARCHITECTURE in a one-person, AI-assisted Product Studio.

Context
- The Studio may build software, SaaS, digital products, websites, AI-enabled products, professional services, and occasional high-trust creative/spatial work.
- One human owns final commercial decisions. AI can analyse, structure, draft, critique, and prepare options, but cannot set a final price, make an external commitment, negotiate, send a proposal, collect payment, make a legal/tax/financial conclusion, or claim an outcome without explicit human approval.
- The goal is not a conventional marketing department or a generic sales framework. The goal is reusable judgment that helps define a truthful, deliverable offer.
- Existing capabilities already cover product prioritisation, technical feasibility, professional-service positioning, copywriting, copy-editing, sales collateral, customer/market evidence, and prospect research. Do not duplicate those jobs.

Research question
What decision method should an AI use to prepare and critique an offer so that the offer is clear, valuable, deliverable, ethically represented, commercially bounded, and reversible when evidence is weak?

Research the following separately for productised services, bespoke professional services, and digital/SaaS products. Do not assume their mechanics are identical.

1. The offer as a decision object
   - audience/context and problem/job;
   - desired change and realistic value;
   - deliverable, method, scope, inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, timeline, responsibilities, and acceptance boundary;
   - alternatives, non-fit, and the truthful next step.
2. Packaging and scope design
   - when options/tiers help versus confuse;
   - how to prevent scope ambiguity, unbounded revisions, hidden dependencies, and value promise drift;
   - how to distinguish an offer, proposal, statement of work, product pricing page, and contract without giving legal advice.
3. Price hypothesis preparation
   - evidence and decision frames that can inform a human price decision: value, willingness to pay, delivery cost/capacity, risk, comparable alternatives, fixed versus variable scope, and buyer uncertainty;
   - why an AI must not turn a formula into a final price or guarantee ROI;
   - what uncertainty, sensitivity, and approval notes must accompany any price range or option.
4. Proof and claims
   - how delivery capability, demonstrations, case evidence, testimonials, outcomes, comparative claims, and confidence limits should constrain an offer;
   - common ways offers overpromise or use prestige, scarcity, or false certainty instead of proof.
5. Failure modes and disconfirmation
   - evidence that an offer should be narrowed, tested cheaply, deferred, merged back into a positioning decision, or rejected;
   - indicators that a standalone offer-architecture skill would be unnecessary or harmful.

Evidence rules
- Prioritise primary and authoritative sources: original research, recognised standards bodies, university/academic publications, official consumer-protection guidance, original authors/publishers for books and methods, and credible practitioner material with disclosed basis.
- Search widely, including books, academic work, credible practitioner writing, official guidance, public case studies, and original methodology documents. For books behind paywalls, cite the bibliographic source and clearly distinguish verified content from secondary discussion. Do not fabricate quotations or claim access to a book you cannot inspect.
- Do not rely on social-media threads, generic SEO blogs, or sales-framework summaries as evidence. You may mention a named framework only after identifying its origin, intended context, evidence limits, and why it should remain optional rather than compulsory.
- Separate durable principles from channel-specific tactics, country-specific legal requirements, vendor advice, and dated market claims.
- Treat Nigeria and any country-specific questions as context requiring later qualified review, not as a basis for legal or financial conclusions.

Required deliverable
Return a decision report, not a skill. Include:
1. Executive conclusion: whether a standalone Offer Architecture capability is justified.
2. A blank-sheet capability map: decisions, inputs, outputs, owner, activation, exclusions, handoffs, failure modes, and verification evidence.
3. A distinction between universal principles, conditional heuristics, and advice that should be rejected or deferred.
4. A proposed minimal decision record for an offer, including a human-approved price-hypothesis field and a claim/proof ledger.
5. A map of what belongs in the future skill entrypoint versus conditional references versus templates versus a workflow.
6. Clear boundaries with product thinking, expert positioning, copywriting, sales enablement, engineering, design, and qualified legal/financial review.
7. At least five concrete anti-patterns and their safer alternative.
8. A source table with title, author/publisher, date, source type, direct URL where available, what it supports, and limitations.
9. A disconfirmation section: what future evidence would show this should not be a standalone skill.

Do not write the final skill. Do not propose autonomous outreach, price changes, proposal sending, payment collection, advertising spend, contracts, or external actions.
```

## Brief B — AI Governance, Evidence, Handoffs, and Commercial Decision State

```text
Conduct a rigorous, source-backed research study on how an AI-assisted one-person Product Studio should govern OFFER ARCHITECTURE and consequential commercial decisions.

Context
- The Studio uses capable AI agents, skills, references, workflows, and optional specialist packs. A human remains accountable for external commitments.
- The Studio needs a capability that prepares commercial decisions for software, websites, digital products, professional services, and occasional high-trust creative work.
- Existing capabilities already own product decisions, technical feasibility, interface design, testing, copy, positioning, sales collateral, customer/market evidence, and prospect research.
- This study must identify what an AI offer-preparation capability may do internally, what it must label as uncertain, what it must hand off, and when a stateful workflow is genuinely required.

Research question
What evidence, provenance, uncertainty, approval, and handoff design prevents an AI-assisted offer process from creating unsupported claims, unsafe commitments, false precision, or bureaucracy—while still being genuinely useful to a one-person Studio?

Research the following:

1. Decision evidence and claim discipline
   - how to distinguish verified fact, attributed statement, interpretation, working hypothesis, recommendation, and unknown;
   - provenance, date, context, limitation, and approval-state fields that materially reduce commercial risk;
   - treatment of testimonials, case studies, comparative claims, performance/ROI claims, customer research, willingness-to-pay signals, and AI-generated content.
2. AI and human authority
   - useful human-in-the-loop controls for decision preparation versus external commitment;
   - how to prevent draft-to-commitment confusion, automation bias, fabricated proof, unauthorised contact, covert profiling, or AI setting prices;
   - how to keep approval gates precise instead of placing everything behind a vague “human review.”
3. Cross-functional handoffs
   - the smallest useful handoff packet between commercial/product strategy, engineering, design, quality/testing, and the human owner;
   - how feasibility, delivery capacity, accessibility, security, evidence, and release constraints should be incorporated without turning offer work into a waterfall process.
4. When a workflow is justified
   - distinguish a normal direct copy or positioning task from a stateful commercial-decision record;
   - define minimum lifecycle states and evidence for a new paid offer, material repositioning, major proposal, material scope change, or human-reviewed price change;
   - define a lightweight conditional learning loop for a live product/page with a meaningful decision, observable evidence, a reversible intervention, result interpretation, and a decision archive;
   - explain why low traffic, weak instrumentation, or purely qualitative evidence should not be presented as causal proof.
5. Privacy, fairness, and external boundaries
   - public/approved-data limits; sensitive-attribute inference; recruiting, data collection, contact, publication, advertising, payment, and CRM boundaries;
   - identify the difference between portable governance principles and local legal advice. Do not provide country-specific legal conclusions.

Evidence rules
- Prioritise original standards, primary research, official regulator/standards guidance, academic publications, and original documentation. Search broadly but report the provenance and limitations of non-primary sources.
- Search books, original frameworks, academic literature, responsible-AI guidance, privacy/consumer-protection guidance, and real-world operations material. Do not invent quotations, legal duties, or access to paywalled material.
- Treat named frameworks as optional candidates, not mandatory rituals. Reject advice that assumes a large sales team, high traffic, a CRM operation, paid acquisition, or autonomous external actions.
- Separate evidence-backed principles from recommendations that are reasonable but not strongly established.

Required deliverable
Return a decision report, not a skill or code. Include:
1. A minimum evidence and claim ledger with field definitions and examples.
2. A human/AI authority matrix: permitted internal preparation, required approval, prohibited actions, and specialist handoffs.
3. A minimal commercial-decision-record workflow contract: activation, exclusions, inputs, states, approval gates, outputs, verification, and failure paths.
4. A minimal conditional live-learning-loop workflow contract with a no-data/low-data path.
5. A handoff-packet template for Product Strategy, Design, Engineering, Quality, and the human owner.
6. Anti-patterns, failure modes, and disconfirmation criteria.
7. A source table with title, author/publisher, date, source type, URL where available, precise claim supported, and limitation.
8. Clear recommendations for what belongs in a future `offer-architecture` skill, what belongs in a workflow, what belongs in shared references/templates, and what should be deferred.

Do not write the final skill. Do not authorize external contact, publishing, advertising, payment, contract acceptance, pricing changes, CRM mutation, or sensitive-data collection.
```

## Synthesis gate

Do not create the skill when only one report returns. Compare both reports against:

1. the Growth department decision report;
2. current `product-thinking`, `expert-positioning`, `copywriting`, `sales-enablement`, `research-analysis`, and `page-cro` boundaries;
3. the Studio’s authority model; and
4. whether the proposed method adds reusable judgement rather than a sales ritual.

Only then decide the smallest package: entrypoint, selective references, templates, evals, and workflow handoffs.
