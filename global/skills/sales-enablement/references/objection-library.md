# Evidence-Backed Objection Support

Load this reference for internal preparation after an actual objection, a documented buyer concern, or a clearly labelled hypothesis. Do not use it to manipulate, pressure, or misrepresent.

## Build an Objection Record

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Exact wording and source | Quote or summarise with date, call, email, or other source. |
| Status | Observed concern, client-reported concern, or working hypothesis. |
| Context | Buyer role, deal stage, decision, scope, and known constraints. |
| Evidence | Relevant capability, comparison, price, case proof, limitation, or unknown. |
| Safe response | Acknowledge; state only supported facts; name a limitation; ask a useful validating question. |
| Escalation | Evidence, authority, or specialist review needed before a further claim. |

## Response Pattern

1. Acknowledge the concern without pretending to know its hidden cause.
2. State the supported answer and any material limitation.
3. Ask a question that checks priority, scope, evidence, authority, or alternative.
4. Agree a next step or record the unresolved gap.

## Examples

| Concern | Safe response shape |
| --- | --- |
| "We already use X." | Ask what works and what is missing; compare only a documented, decision-relevant dimension. |
| "We do not have budget." | Clarify whether the issue is amount, timing, authority, priority, or value confidence. Do not invent savings or discounts. |
| "We are too small." | Ask about the actual workflow, users, and desired outcome. Describe supported fit and constraints. |
| "Can you guarantee it?" | State that outcomes depend on named conditions; route guarantee terms to the authorised human owner. |

## Do Not Use

- Do not use a persona card as proof of a buyer's motive.
- Do not turn a competitor stereotype into a comparative claim.
- Do not claim scarcity, consensus, or urgency without current evidence and approval.
