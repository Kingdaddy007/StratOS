# Claim Governance and Approval

Load this reference when a sales asset may affect a buyer decision or create an external effect. Do not treat it as legal advice. Verify current jurisdictional, sector, platform, contractual, and client-specific obligations before using them.

## Claim Ledger

Create an entry for each material statement.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Claim ID and exact wording | Include headings, charts, image captions, footnotes, calls to action, and price labels. |
| Class | Verified fact; supplied fact; client assertion; hypothesis; example; estimate; benchmark; illustrative scenario; testimonial; comparative claim; unsupported claim. |
| Evidence and boundary | Source, owner, date, method, scope, market, product/version, population, timeframe, and conditions. |
| Interpretation | What a reasonable intended audience could infer, including implications and omissions. |
| Confidence and consequence | Confidence is not proof. Record the decision the statement could influence. |
| Permission and treatment | Permission/usage constraint plus publish, qualify, label, hold, seek evidence, or escalate. |

## Classification Rules

| Class | Buyer-facing rule |
| --- | --- |
| Verified fact | State only within its evidence boundary. Recheck volatile claims. |
| Supplied fact or client assertion | Attribute it; do not present it as independently verified. |
| Hypothesis or persona inference | Use it as a discovery question or label it as a hypothesis. |
| Example or illustrative scenario | Label it clearly. Never make it resemble an undocumented client result. |
| Estimate or value model | Show inputs, units, sources, formula, timeframe, omitted costs, uncertainty, and scenario/sensitivity where material. Call it an estimate, never a promise. |
| Benchmark | Identify source, population, date, method, comparability limits, and difference from the buyer's case. |
| Testimonial or review | Confirm authenticity, permission, scope, relationship disclosure where needed, and approval. Preserve meaning. |
| Comparative claim | Define dimension, comparison set, date, scope, and current source. Remove an unsubstantiated superiority implication. |
| Unsupported claim | Keep only as an internal question. Do not publish or send it. |

## Safe Alternatives

| Risky request | Use this instead |
| --- | --- |
| "This redesign will increase conversion by 30%." | "We can provide a conversion review with documented hypotheses and test recommendations." |
| "Only three spots left" without evidence | State an actual deadline or capacity only when it is true and approved; otherwise remove urgency. |
| "Best in the market" | Describe a specific supported capability or ask for a defined, current comparison basis. |
| ROI from guesses | Provide a clearly labelled scenario with inputs, range, and sensitivity, or stop. |
| Client-result claim with no proof | Attribute it as a client assertion and request source, period, baseline, and permission. |

## Approval and Stopping Rules

Stop buyer-facing drafting and ask for evidence, authority, or specialist review when:

- a material claim has no source, owner, date, boundary, or permission;
- language is stronger than the available evidence;
- a result, urgency, testimonial, comparison, price, guarantee, scope, term, or timeline has unclear authority;
- personal-data outreach has an unclear authorised or lawful basis;
- audience, market, or channel makes the relevant rule unclear;
- an asset could expose confidential information or create an irreversible external effect; or
- someone asks to turn an illustration, benchmark, aspiration, or model output into a client result.

For an externally distributed asset, record the exact version, channel, named approver, approval date, sources/assumptions, permissions, and residual uncertainty. Inspect the final rendered asset: visual hierarchy, labels, footnotes, links, forms, subject line, sender, call to action, price display, and omitted qualification.

## Evidence Snapshot

The FTC states that advertising must be truthful and non-deceptive, that material express and implied claims need substantiation, and that an advertisement is evaluated in context, including omissions and visuals. The FTC also provides endorsement guidance about honest experience, substantiation, and material connections. These are U.S. sources, not universal legal advice. Use them as a reasoning baseline; verify applicability before any external use.
