# Composition and Experience Critique

Load when reviewing a hero, section, page, prototype, or multi-section experience for hierarchy, typography, imagery, continuity, and visual resolution.

Use only the dimensions relevant to the live question.

## 1. First encounter

Check:

- What is seen first?
- What is understood first?
- Are those the same thing when they should be?
- What is the primary subject?
- What competes with it?
- Is identity available soon enough?
- Does the initial state need motion?
- What does the first transition promise about the rest of the site?

Do not assume three seconds is a universal timing law. Review the first meaningful encounter.

## 2. Hierarchy

Identify:

- dominant element;
- secondary support;
- tertiary information;
- actions / navigation;
- proof.

Ask:

- Can the eye find the intended order?
- Are two elements fighting for dominance?
- Is important information visually too quiet?
- Does motion temporarily rewrite the hierarchy?

## 3. Grid and spatial structure

Check:

- alignment relationships;
- intentional breaks;
- container logic;
- image / text edges;
- rhythm across sections;
- negative-space role;
- viewport dependence.

Do not enforce a grid for its own sake.

A broken grid may be more coherent than a rigid one when the relationships are intentional.

## 4. Spacing

Ask what a gap is doing:

- grouping;
- separation;
- pause;
- framing;
- tension;
- transition.

Avoid both mechanical equal-spacing and arbitrary “designer spacing.”

Inspect optical relationships.

## 5. Typography

Review:

- role;
- hierarchy;
- family / weight contrast;
- line length;
- wrap;
- leading;
- tracking;
- scale;
- placement;
- image relationship;
- responsive state.

Typography may be loud, quiet, oversized, restrained, decorative, utilitarian, or image-like if the concept supports it.

## 6. Imagery

Review:

- why this image is here;
- subject;
- crop;
- scale;
- evidence value;
- image family;
- project identity;
- text collision;
- repetition;
- quality.

For spatial work, protect geometry, light, material, and room relationships.

## 7. Motion in composition

Ask:

- Does movement clarify hierarchy?
- Does it compete with reading / inspection?
- Does a recurring gesture create expectation usefully?
- Are still moments intentionally still?
- Does the movement preserve the subject?

Detailed motion redesign belongs to `cinematic-motion`.

## 8. Section relationship

Adjacent sections should not all need a transition effect.

Check:

- meaning handoff;
- visual handoff;
- intentional contrast;
- repetition;
- chapter weight;
- proof pacing.

A hard cut can be an authored transition.

## 9. Downstream continuity

For whole-site concepts, inspect at least one later representative moment when available.

Ask:

- Which rule from the opening continues?
- Which rule changes?
- Does the site become generic after the hero?
- Is every later section forced to imitate the hero?
- Does proof have a calmer mode?
- Does the ending belong to the same experience?

## 10. Mobile / short viewport

Check:

- subject crop;
- type wrap;
- overlap;
- navigation;
- reading order;
- motion equivalence;
- project labels;
- touch behaviour where relevant.

Desktop elegance does not prove mobile composition.

## 11. Inquiry / action

Critique visual readiness, not business strategy.

Ask:

- Can the action be found?
- Does it compete with the work?
- Is the preceding experience sufficient to make the next step understandable?
- Is the form / link usable?

Commercial qualification belongs elsewhere.

## 12. Focused review format

For a bounded review, report:

1. strongest working relationship;
2. highest-consequence unresolved relationship;
3. evidence;
4. smallest correction / test;
5. what should remain untouched.

Do not produce a checklist dump.
