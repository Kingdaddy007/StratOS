# Page Grammar and Fingerprint

Use this reference when several spatial sites may reuse one production chassis,
when concept territories risk sharing the same page skeleton, or when a new
direction must be compared with earlier work.

## Define the grammar

Name the structural rule that organizes the whole page. Do not mistake palette,
type, imagery, or an animation preset for grammar.

Record:

- **Grammar:** how the visitor moves through and understands the page.
- **Opening:** the first structural event, not only the hero styling.
- **Navigation model:** how orientation and inquiry remain reachable.
- **Act shape:** continuous, chaptered, editorial, gallery, split-stage,
  transformation-led, or a project-specific alternative.
- **Close:** how proof resolves into inquiry.
- **Signature move:** one memorable interaction, transition, or structural
  behavior that belongs to this brand.
- **Forbids:** patterns that would collapse this grammar into a competing
  template or contradict the controlling argument.

Treat grammar names as working vocabulary, not a compulsory catalogue. Invent a
project-specific grammar when the brief supports it.

## Grammar forbids

For every territory, name the mechanics, section behavior, or visual habits it
forbids. Reject rules based only on taste. Each forbid must protect the
controlling argument, proof sequence, accessibility, asset reality, or
maintenance boundary.

Examples:

| Grammar direction | Useful forbid | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet chaptered editorial | Persistent cursor theatre or continuous world camera | Preserve reading, project proof, and calm authority |
| Filmic threshold | Repeated numbered cards and equal-height chapter tiles | Preserve one continuous cinematic argument |
| Transformation reveal | Decorative parallax unrelated to before/after evidence | Keep attention on the design decision and result |
| Material library | Generic full-screen video between every chapter | Preserve tactile comparison and material specificity |

## Maintain a workspace fingerprint ledger

Use `.agents/contexts/site-fingerprints.md` or an approved equivalent only in a
workspace that coordinates multiple site builds or deliberately creates a
reusable production chassis. Keep client secrets and personal data out of the
ledger.

Compare a proposed direction with every relevant prior row across six
dimensions:

1. page grammar;
2. navigation model;
3. opening form;
4. act or chapter shape;
5. closing/inquiry form; and
6. signature move.

Treat fewer than four materially different dimensions as a similarity warning,
not mathematical proof of copying. Revise the direction or record an
evidence-backed exception. Passing the count does not establish brand fit or
quality; the brief and visible rough test remain authoritative.

Add a row only after a direction is selected. Preserve prior rows. When a
project changes materially, append a dated revision or explicitly supersede the
old row without erasing the historical comparison.

## Reusable chassis boundary

Reuse engineering infrastructure without reusing creative identity.

Keep reusable:

- semantic structure and accessibility foundations;
- responsive and loading infrastructure;
- gallery, asset, motion-lifecycle, and verification utilities;
- component primitives whose appearance and behavior remain configurable.

Re-decide per brand:

- diagnosis and creative brief;
- page grammar and forbids;
- narrative/proof sequence;
- opening, close, and signature move;
- type, color, crop, material, density, and motion posture.

If swapping only the logo and assets leaves the same experience argument,
record a fingerprint collision and return to concept territory work.
