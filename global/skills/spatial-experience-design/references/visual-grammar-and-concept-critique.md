# Visual Grammar and Concept Critique

Load when a spatial concept must be invented, compared, extended past the hero, or challenged for sameness.

Use these prompts to produce evidence and decisions, not a mandatory worksheet.

## 1. Find a generative lead

Inspect what is strongest in the real material:

- **Relationship** — people around a founder, objects around a ritual, rooms around a principle.
- **Contrast / transformation** — before/after, rough/refined, dark/lit, detail/full space.
- **Sequence** — approach, threshold, reveal, inspection, understanding, decision.
- **Geometry / rhythm** — openings, grids, curves, joints, repeated spacing, project cadence.
- **Material** — stone, textile, light, surface, reflection, craft.
- **Personality** — theatrical, exacting, playful, intimate, direct, restrained, or another supported quality.
- **Stillness / absence** — space, pause, restraint, withheld information, deliberate emptiness.

Let a visual spark lead when useful. Ask later what visitors understand from it, what it demands from the source, and whether it belongs to the project.

A visually compelling experiment may remain a learning study.

## 2. Compare candidates at the level that matters

For each serious direction, show:

1. **Basis** — fact, interpretation, or creative hypothesis.
2. **Subject** — what visitors look at first and why.
3. **First event** — still or moving; what changes in perception.
4. **Continuation** — first handoff + one later chapter.
5. **Grammar seed** — composition, type, imagery, transition, continuity, intentional stillness.
6. **Demand** — assets, content, responsive changes, fallback, cost.
7. **Failure** — what makes it misleading, artificial, generic, or too expensive.

Compare **subject, visitor journey, proof timing, and visual operations**.

Colour changes or different hero effects on the same structure are not different territories.

Use the fewest candidates that expose the real choice.

If the evidence does not yet distinguish candidates, state the deciding uncertainty instead of inventing certainty.

## 3. Externalize selectively

Prototype the part most likely to disprove the idea.

Examples:

- layered scene → test occlusion, seams, contact shadows, reverse scroll;
- panoramic journey → test available views + mobile composition;
- material story → test whether detail genuinely resolves into a complete project;
- collection → test project identity and chapter boundaries;
- generated sequence → test geometry / material continuity;
- typography-over-image → test real crops and legibility;
- unusual transition → test whether the second section inherits a useful rule or just receives a trick.

Do not spend equal effort on every candidate.

## 4. Extend the selected idea into a grammar

Define only relevant rules.

| Question | Possible decision |
| --- | --- |
| **How does it open?** | Immediate hero, still title state, typographic entrance, threshold, material close-up, cinematic sequence, or another justified state |
| **What is seen?** | Space, object, decision, collection, practice, context, or project-specific subject |
| **How is it viewed?** | Still, crop travel, pan, dolly, track, orbit, comparative frame, changing light, hard cut |
| **How does type behave?** | Hold, reveal, occlude, scale, annotate, drift, reflow, or stay quiet |
| **How do images behave?** | Full bleed, framed plate, sequence, expanding crop, still proof, scrubbed media, paired comparison |
| **How do chapters connect?** | Camera direction, material, frame edge, type cadence, object, light, deliberate cut, or no visual transition |
| **Where does it pause?** | Proof reading, project inspection, navigation, inquiry, orientation |
| **How should interaction feel?** | Tactile, precise, editorial, playful, direct, exploratory, cinematic, restrained, or another justified character |
| **Where is intensity spent?** | Opening, one signature chapter, transformation, portfolio shift, close, or deliberately nowhere |
| **What changes on mobile / reduced motion?** | Equivalent meaning through crop, order, still frame, manual control, simpler transition |

A recurring gesture can become identity. Repeating it without a chapter job becomes a preset.

A later section may be nearly motionless even when the opening is cinematic.

## 5. Use an intensity budget

Ask:

- What is the primary remembered moment?
- Which supporting moments need enough motion to preserve continuity?
- Where should visitors be allowed to inspect work without choreography?
- Does every section compete for attention?
- Would removing one major effect make the hierarchy stronger?
- Is the level of spectacle justified by the brand, visitor, and asset quality?

Do not equate a low intensity budget with luxury or a high budget with innovation.

## 6. Critique in order

1. **Truth** — Does the site show work and authority the practice actually has?
2. **Subject** — Does the treatment help visitors understand the real work?
3. **Visitor** — Can people orient, read, inspect, trust, and act?
4. **Continuity** — Can the governing idea survive a meaningful later chapter?
5. **Composition** — Do crop, type, light, spacing, and hierarchy work in the actual viewport?
6. **Effort** — Does media / motion earn its asset, performance, accessibility, and maintenance cost?
7. **Distinctiveness** — Would the experience still belong to this brand if the admired reference and luxury styling disappeared?

State what the available mockup, source, browser state, or prototype proves—and what remains untested.

## 7. Watch for repeated site grammar

When several client sites share a production chassis, compare:

- opening form;
- navigation behaviour;
- hero subject;
- chapter shape;
- proof rhythm;
- signature interaction;
- transition logic;
- close / inquiry.

Shared code is acceptable. Shared creative arguments across unrelated brands deserve scrutiny.

Do not use a difference count as a quality score. Ask whether each site's governing form follows its own material and visitor task.
