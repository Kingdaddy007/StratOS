# Spatial Outreach Site Sprint

## WHEN TO USE THIS

- Build a private, prospect-specific spatial website concept from public evidence, supplied assets, and an authorised local repository.
- Compress a qualifying `spatial-project-inception` into one delegated production run while retaining evidence, authorship, prototype, accessibility, and verification gates.
- Create a strong autonomous first pass that the user can review, refine, record, and present without pretending the first generation is automatically final.

## NEVER DO

- Never promise a true zero-iteration masterpiece, a reply, a sale, or a client outcome.
- Never invent projects, clients, testimonials, services, process claims, awards, metrics, founder beliefs, or portfolio scale.
- Never begin from a background effect, page template, section stack, or animation library before defining the prospect thesis and opening fixation.
- Never clone the page grammar, signature motion, visual thesis, or section architecture of an earlier site.
- Never split independent section design across workers before one shared thesis, experience sequence, asset boundary, and design system exist.
- Never install dependencies, import third-party source, generate paid media, deploy, publish, upload prospect assets, or send outreach without the separate authority required for that effect.
- Never call a source check, screenshot, or build command complete website verification.
- Never let desktop spectacle conceal weak mobile composition, reduced-motion behavior, proof, inquiry, accessibility, or performance.

## OUTCOME CONTRACT

Produce a private, working, prospect-specific concept that demonstrates one respectful perception shift. Treat the run as **AI augmentation with bounded autonomy**: automate evidence organization, territory generation, reversible concept selection, implementation, and self-review; retain the user's control over taste, public use, outreach, commercial claims, and final acceptance.

Define before broad production:

1. **Observed condition:** What public evidence and supplied assets show.
2. **Respectful hypothesis:** What may be difficult to perceive in the current presentation.
3. **Desired revaluation:** What a visitor should understand, feel, believe, and do differently.
4. **Visual thesis:** One governing spatial-editorial argument for the whole page.
5. **Proof spine:** Which real projects, decisions, materials, process evidence, and authority cues earn the claim.
6. **Honest limit:** What the private concept cannot establish before discovery.

Reject a thesis that could be transferred unchanged to another prospect.

## SPRINT ROUTE

Use the `spatial-project-inception` workflow's private speculative sprint lane. Answer its four working questions in a concise project brief and production/verification plan when separate files would create ceremony rather than decision clarity.

### 1. Orient and inventory

- Inspect the repository, applicable instructions, existing implementation, public evidence supplied or explicitly authorised for inspection, and every usable asset.
- Record `FACT`, `REPORTED`, `INFERENCE`, and `UNKNOWN` separately.
- Classify assets by source, project relationship, orientation, resolution, crop flexibility, rights uncertainty, and likely narrative job.
- Preserve the existing stack and accepted strengths unless a documented blocker requires a change.

### 2. Frame the outreach and experience thesis

- Use `speculative-outreach` when the Growth pack is active to define the observed condition, perception gap, desired revaluation, demonstration, honest limit, and later binary next step.
- Use `brand-strategy`, `spatial-experience-design`, and `storytelling` to turn that thesis into one authored experience rather than a list of sections.
- State what must be known in the first three seconds, what proof follows, where the remembered peak occurs, and why inquiry becomes appropriate.
- Define one **living continuity system** that makes the website feel like a changing spatial world rather than disconnected sections. Keep its communication job stable while allowing its geometry, material, lighting, motion, and implementation technique to be prospect-specific.

### 3. Diverge lightly, then select

- Generate three structurally different whole-page territories in compact form. Include a restrained or still-led territory unless the evidence makes it dishonest.
- Give each territory a governing argument, opening form, proof sequence, living continuity system, asset burden, motion posture, mobile risk, and failure condition.
- Query the local Design-Audit library only after naming the decision and reference questions. Load the smallest relevant reports; record `KEEP`, `ADAPT`, `REJECT`, and `DEFER`, plus what is intentionally not copied.
- Use `master-design-director` to select one territory against brand truth, authorship, first fixation, proof, full-page potential, asset reality, responsiveness, accessibility, performance, and maintainability.
- When the user explicitly delegated private reversible creative choices, self-select and record the rejected options plus reversal condition. Do not misrepresent this as client approval.

### 4. Prototype the dangerous assumption

- Build the riskiest mechanic or relationship first, then one representative vertical slice with real-enough type, imagery, copy, mobile behavior, and static fallback.
- Treat the opening plus the first proof transition as the default slice when no other risk dominates.
- Return to the thesis, simplify, or choose a different territory when the slice is attractive but unauthored, technically fragile, asset-hungry, inaccessible, or generic.

### 5. Produce as one sequence

- Expand only after the slice earns an `expand` verdict.
- Build by coherent narrative slices, not by handing unrelated sections to independent designers.
- Preserve one grid logic, typographic behavior, crop system, image-light relationship, proof spine, motion grammar, and inquiry posture across the page.
- Use copy to reveal the prospect's actual point of view and proof. Remove generic luxury language and explanatory duplication.

### 6. Cold-scroll audit and correction

- Inspect the live page as one authored sequence at desktop and mobile widths.
- Audit visual thesis, first three seconds, eye path, grid, alignment, typography, typographic color, copy-image relationship, crop and lighting continuity, motion jobs and timing, pacing, transitions, inquiry readiness, premium leaks, and subtraction.
- Rank only `Must Fix`, `Should Fix`, and `Could Improve`; correct the high-confidence Must Fix set before delivery.
- Verify reduced motion as an equivalent static composition, not merely disabled transitions.

### 7. Evidence handoff

- Run the project-native production build and relevant tests.
- Verify desktop, mobile, reduced motion, keyboard/focus behavior when interactive, media/fallback behavior, overflow, browser console health, and asset loading.
- Report what was actually checked, what it proves, changed files, residual uncertainty, and the next human decision.
- Keep deployment, public sharing, walkthrough upload, and outreach sending as separate approval-gated actions.

## LIVING CONTINUITY SIGNATURE

Require every sprint to author one **living continuity system**. It must remain perceptible across at least three narrative chapters, change state as meaning advances, and help the page read as one spatial experience. Define its job after the thesis and before broad production; do not let a tool or effect choose the concept.

Create three structurally different continuity candidates before selection. Vary the world law, geometry, material, lighting, state changes, interaction, and remembered peak—not only colour or effect intensity. At least one candidate must prove that the signature can survive without Canvas/WebGL.

Choose among still/image compositing, typography, CSS/DOM, pre-rendered media, SVG, Canvas/WebGL, persistent objects, light/material fields, or a hybrid. Select `canvas-ui` only when one contained real-time system carries the job better than the lighter alternatives. Require:

- a concise world-law sentence and chapter-state map;
- a semantic DOM/static equivalent;
- readable text-safe states across the full sequence;
- mobile and reduced-motion fallbacks;
- lifecycle, cleanup, resize, and performance boundaries;
- an isolated risk prototype before whole-page integration.

When earlier sites or fingerprints are available, reject any candidate that repeats a prior site's dominant geometry, opening event, lighting language, reveal pattern, transition behaviour, motion curve, chapter order, or remembered peak. Preserve the signature principle—living continuity—while changing its expression.

Reject a continuity system that competes with project proof, makes every scene share one visual volume, or becomes the only thing remembered about the prospect.

## DELEGATION RULES

Use the parent Studio Director and functional leads as the ownership system. Do not create a permanent custom agent for this sprint.

Delegate only after the shared brief exists, and only for disjoint, independently verifiable lanes such as:

- evidence or asset inventory with provenance;
- bounded image preparation or approved media-production candidates;
- a contained motion/Canvas risk prototype in owned files;
- independent browser, accessibility, responsive, or console verification.

Give every worker exact ownership, mutation ceiling, evidence requirement, and return contract. Keep final creative integration with the Design Director/parent. Keep independent assurance outside the implementer's worker tree when the host exposes that separation.

## ANTI-PATTERNS

| Anti-Pattern | What It Is | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-Shot Fantasy | Treating one generation as guaranteed final quality | Run one autonomous production loop with internal prototypes, browser review, correction, and human acceptance |
| Background-First Design | Building a Canvas or gradient world before the argument | Fix the thesis, fixation, proof spine, and continuity job before choosing technique |
| Section Swarm | Assigning hero, portfolio, process, and footer to separate agents immediately | Establish one blueprint and system; delegate only isolated evidence, risk, or QA lanes |
| Prior-Site Clone | Reusing the same signature reveal, chapter order, or motion grammar | Preserve the quality rubric while generating prospect-specific page grammar and forbids |
| Portfolio Theater | Creating breadth or authority the evidence does not support | Curate fewer real assets and expose honest limits |
| Motion Abundance | Equating more transitions with higher quality | Give each motion one semantic job; preserve a complete still experience |
| Verification Theater | Reporting a build pass as proof of the experience | Inspect the running page across viewport, motion, interaction, console, and asset states |

## OUTPUT SHAPE

Return:

1. **Sprint thesis:** observation, hypothesis, desired revaluation, visual thesis, proof spine, honest limit.
2. **Direction record:** selected territory, rejected alternatives, reversal condition, background/motion decision.
3. **Implemented concept:** repository path, running scope, changed files, intentionally omitted scope.
4. **Director verdict:** Must Fix, Should Fix, Could Improve, and what was removed.
5. **Evidence:** desktop, mobile, reduced motion, interaction, console, assets, tests, and build actually checked.
6. **Human handoff:** remaining taste decisions, unsupported claims, rights/source uncertainty, and the next safe action.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE CHECKLIST

1. Ground the concept in prospect-specific evidence and honest limits.
2. Define the argument and first fixation before the continuity technique or motion.
3. Compare three lightweight whole-page territories and three distinct living continuity systems before self-selection.
4. Prototype the riskiest assumption and representative slice before broad production.
5. Preserve one authored sequence across copy, image, type, grid, proof, motion, and inquiry.
6. Use a new page grammar rather than cloning prior work.
7. Make mobile and reduced motion complete compositions.
8. Correct high-confidence Must Fix issues in the live implementation.
9. Verify the running result and production build proportionately.
10. Leave publication, paid generation, external messaging, and commercial acceptance with the user.
