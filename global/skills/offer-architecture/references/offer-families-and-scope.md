# Offer Families and Scope Boundaries

Load this reference when a material offer needs packaging, scope, dependency, or acceptance judgement.

## Select the offer family

| Family | Commercial object | Emphasise | Do not imply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Productised service | Bounded human-led package | Eligibility, fixed deliverables, timebox, revision/change rule, capacity, exception handling | Every buyer gets the same result or standardisation removes judgement. |
| Bespoke professional service | Adapted judgement and work for a specific context | Discovery limit, assumptions, responsibilities, review windows, change handling, acceptance | Discovery solves every unknown or the buyer receives an outcome guarantee. |
| Digital/SaaS product | Ongoing access to a product capability | Features, usage/seats, support, integrations, data/security boundaries, availability limits, roadmap uncertainty | A pricing page is a contract or access promises custom work. |

Treat this as a choice of scope mechanics, not an identity label. A proposal may contain more than one family only when the boundaries are explicit.

## Scope-control checks

Define observable boundaries before persuasive wording:

1. List deliverables and the level of completion expected.
2. List exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, buyer inputs, and third-party conditions.
3. State responsibility and decision rights for reviews, feedback, content, access, approvals, and changes.
4. Use a range when timing depends on unresolved inputs. Do not promise a deadline whose dependencies are unknown.
5. Define completion through observable criteria, not an uncontrolled commercial outcome.
6. Use a new decision record for material change. Do not silently absorb scope to appear helpful.

## Packaging choices

Use options only when they differ materially in at least one of: scope, risk, pace, support, access, responsibility, or buyer fit. A single bounded offer, diagnostic, pilot, or non-fit may be clearer than a tiered menu.

| Pattern | Use when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One bounded package | Inputs and delivery are repeatable enough to state a clear boundary. | Important requirements or dependencies are still unknown. |
| Staged diagnostic or pilot | A full commitment would hide uncertainty; early evidence can change the later scope. | It is used merely to delay a decision the Studio already understands. |
| Material options | Options change the actual work, risk envelope, support, or speed. | Tiers only decorate the same work or create false buyer choice. |
| Non-fit or referral | The buyer need, risk, capacity, or dependency sits outside the Studio’s credible boundary. | It is used as manufactured exclusivity rather than an honest limit. |

## Document boundary

- An **offer** explains the commercial object and decision logic.
- A **proposal** presents a human-reviewed recommendation to a particular buyer.
- A **statement of work** describes execution boundaries and evidence; it may require qualified review.
- A **pricing page** communicates approved packages, limits, and next steps.
- A **contract** creates legal obligations; do not treat AI-generated language as legally sufficient.
