# Evidence, Price Hypotheses, and Claims

Load this reference for material proof, public claims, testimonials, comparisons, outcome language, price scenarios, or any offer where uncertainty could change a human decision.

## Label the material statement

| Label | Meaning | Required treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fact | Verified first-party or reliable-source statement | Retain source, scope, date, and any relevant condition. |
| Attributed statement | Someone’s reported claim or testimonial | Preserve attribution, context, permission, and limits. |
| Interpretation | Reasoned reading of evidence | Name the supporting evidence and plausible alternatives. |
| Hypothesis | Testable possibility, including AI-generated wording | State what would support, weaken, or disconfirm it. |
| Recommendation | Proposed action | Name the decision owner, trade-off, and approval required. |
| Unknown | Unestablished information | Keep visible; do not turn it into convincing language. |

Do not let a case study, model output, buyer statement, willingness-to-pay response, or observational result become a universal outcome promise.

## Prepare a price hypothesis

Prepare options or ranges only for an internal human decision. State:

1. The commercial object and offer family.
2. Value signals and their limitations: problem consequence, alternatives, urgency, and controllable change.
3. Delivery economics: capacity, tools, infrastructure, support, exceptions, rework risk, and opportunity cost.
4. Risk/uncertainty: incomplete requirements, access, content/data quality, integration, approvals, buyer actions, and third parties.
5. Alternatives: smaller diagnostic, staged option, another provider, internal solution, doing nothing, defer, or non-fit.
6. Scenarios, assumptions, sensitivities, evidence strength, unknowns, and a revisit trigger.
7. Status: `internal scenario`, `hypothesis`, or `externally approved`.

Never output an exact figure merely because a formula can produce one. Never use a price calculation as a prediction of value, return, conversion, or demand.

## Claim checks

Before a material external claim, verify that the words do not imply more substantiation than the available evidence supports. For objective performance, comparative, testimonial, outcome, safety, or ROI language:

- retain the source and its conditions;
- narrow the wording to what the evidence actually establishes;
- name the geography, audience, time period, or method where their absence misleads;
- seek action-specific human approval before publication, sending, advertising, or another external effect; and
- route legal, finance, privacy, accessibility, security, or technical conclusions to the qualified owner.

The FTC's U.S. substantiation guidance is a useful portable principle: the strength of an objective advertising claim must be matched by an appropriate reasonable basis before dissemination. It is not a global legal conclusion. See the source rechecked 2026-08-19: <https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation>.

## Authority and evidence limits

AI may organise evidence, compare scenarios, identify contradictions, draft a record, and prepare questions. AI may not set a price, approve a claim, infer sensitive attributes, turn a draft into a commitment, or execute an external action. Make the human decision and the evidence gap explicit rather than masking them with confidence.
