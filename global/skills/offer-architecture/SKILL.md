# Offer Architecture

## WHEN TO USE THIS

- Prepare or challenge a new paid offer, a material repositioning, a major proposal, a material scope change, or a price hypothesis that needs human review.
- Turn an already-informed audience, problem, and delivery reality into a bounded commercial object: offer, scope, proof, assumptions, price scenarios, and next step.
- Identify when the honest next step is a diagnostic, pilot, narrower option, product decision, specialist check, or non-fit rather than a full commitment.

## NEVER DO

- Never set, publish, discount, or change a final price; prepare clearly labelled scenarios for human decision only.
- Never turn desired business outcomes, ROI, conversion, revenue, prestige, security, accessibility, or compliance into a promise without context-matched evidence and approval.
- Never present an offer, proposal, pricing page, statement of work, or contract as interchangeable or legally sufficient.
- Never send a proposal, contact a buyer, collect data, accept payment, negotiate, mutate a CRM, or create another external effect.
- Never invent demand, willingness to pay, testimonials, customer results, technical feasibility, delivery capacity, or a buyer's intent.
- Never replace a material unknown with decorative packages, rigid tiers, a pricing formula, or false precision.

## CLASSIFY THE DECISION

Keep an ordinary approved copy, formatting, or local design task direct. Activate this capability only when the commercial object changes: what is offered, who it is for, what is included or excluded, what proof supports it, what risk is accepted, what price hypothesis is considered, or what human decision comes next.

1. Name the decision owner, decision, audience/context, and consequence of being wrong.
2. Classify the offer family: `productised service`, `bespoke professional service`, or `digital/SaaS product`.
3. Gather existing positioning, product, technical, design, quality, capacity, and evidence facts. Label facts, attributed statements, interpretations, hypotheses, recommendations, and unknowns.
4. Stop and hand off when the commercial decision is actually a product direction, technical feasibility, legal/financial question, inaccessible experience, security risk, or unverified public claim.

## BUILD THE OFFER OBJECT

Define only what the decision needs:

1. Intended audience/context, problem or job, current alternative, desired change, non-fit cases, and truthful next step.
2. Studio-controlled deliverables and method; separate them from buyer- or market-controlled outcomes.
3. Inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, buyer responsibilities, timeline range, review/change rules, support boundary, and observable acceptance boundary.
4. Proof and claim limits. Match each material promise to a source, scope, date, limitation, and approval state; narrow, attribute, or mark unknown when proof is absent.
5. Price scenarios only when they can state their basis: value signals, cost/capacity, risk, alternatives, buyer uncertainty, sensitivities, and material unknowns. Mark every price as `internal scenario`, `hypothesis`, or `externally approved`; only a human can select the latter.

Use a package or option only when it represents a meaningful difference in scope, risk, speed, support, or buyer fit. When unknowns are material, prefer a bounded diagnostic, pilot, staged decision, or non-fit over decorative tiers or unbounded delivery.

## HANDOFFS AND AUTHORITY

- Use `product-thinking` when the problem, audience, value, or build/defer decision remains unresolved.
- Use `expert-positioning` when the offer would change the strategic audience, category, differentiated promise, or professional-service posture.
- Request Engineering, Design/UI-UX, Security, or Quality/Testing input before making a material delivery, accessibility, security, or acceptance claim in their domain.
- Use `copywriting`, `copy-editing`, and `sales-enablement` only after the offer object and claim boundary are sufficiently clear.
- Use `commercial-decision-record` only for a material decision that needs versioned evidence, specialist challenge, human approval, and an archive. Do not create workflow state for a small direct task.

## OUTPUT SHAPE

For a bounded offer decision, provide:

1. Decision and owner.
2. Offer family, audience/context, problem, desired change, and non-fit.
3. Deliverables, inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, and acceptance boundary.
4. Proof/claim limits and material unknowns.
5. Options or a price hypothesis only when justified, with assumptions and mandatory human approval.
6. Specialist handoffs, approval required, safest next step, and revisit trigger.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE CHECKLIST

1. Keep scope, proof, delivery capability, and price assumptions visible.
2. Separate outputs from outcomes and hypotheses from facts.
3. Choose a staged or narrower path when uncertainty makes a full commitment unsafe.
4. Preserve the authority of product, engineering, design, quality, security, and qualified specialists.
5. Keep any external offer, price, claim, publication, contact, payment, or contract behind explicit human approval.
