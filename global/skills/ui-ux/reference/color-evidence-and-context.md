# Colour Evidence and Context

Load this reference only when a colour decision depends on brand expression,
cultural/site context, psychology, print/projection, data visualisation,
spatial/cinematic conditions, or an experiment claim.

## Evidence Labels

| Label | Use for | Do not use it to claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `required` | Applicable standards or project requirements | Audience preference or brand fit |
| `supported` | Strong official convention or directly relevant evidence | A universal outcome outside its conditions |
| `context-dependent` | Audience, category, culture, scene, or medium-sensitive finding | An individual's preference or a causal business result |
| `conventional` | A practical design convention | Compliance, conversion, or research proof |
| `unresolved` | Missing project evidence or untested hypothesis | A finished decision |

## Brand, Psychology, and Culture

- Treat colour-emotion associations as many-to-many and context-dependent.
  Do not promise that a hue produces trust, luxury, urgency, calm, or purchase.
- Never infer a person's or market's colour preference from gender, age, or
  culture labels. Ask for real audience, category, market, and brand evidence
  when symbolism materially affects the decision.
- Treat colour-wheel relationships as optional ideation prompts. Name the
  actual criterion—hierarchy, legibility, belonging together, figure-ground,
  distinction, or expression—before judging an option.
- Keep colour subordinate to the whole composition: typography, imagery,
  material, copy, spacing, interaction, service context, and proof also shape
  interpretation.

## Specialist Contexts

| Context | Additional questions | Minimum review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data visualisation | Can adjacent series be distinguished without hue alone? | Labels, shapes/patterns, legend/annotation, actual data view |
| Print/projection | What profile, substrate, ink/display, lighting, distance, and proofing path apply? | Actual proof or representative output where material |
| Spatial/cinematic | How do luminance, material, light, glare, movement, distance, and site affect salience? | Scene/display review plus separate functional/wayfinding check |
| Experiment | What hypothesis, primary outcome, guardrails, sample uncertainty, and stopping rule exist? | A valid experiment; never an isolated “CTA colour” anecdote |

## Source Use

Use current primary standards for accessibility questions, official design
systems for token-structure examples, research for bounded hypotheses, and
project evidence for brand or behavioural claims.

- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) — use of colour and contrast
  requirements for web content.
- [W3C contrast minimum guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
  — text pairing conditions and exceptions.
- [W3C non-text contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
  — components, graphical objects, and state indicators.
- [USWDS colour guidance](https://designsystem.digital.gov/design-tokens/color/overview/)
  — semantic system/theme/state token examples.
- [Jonauskaite and Mohr systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39806242/)
  — treat colour/emotion claims as limited and context-sensitive.

Record source date, relevance, and limitations when a research claim changes a
project decision. Recheck standards and provider/tool claims at use time.
