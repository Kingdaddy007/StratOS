# Expert Positioning

## WHEN TO USE THIS

- Audit a professional-service surface that feels generic, vendor-like, unclear, over-broad, or poorly qualified.
- Define an honest audience, problem, alternative set, offer boundary, proof architecture, and inquiry posture for a high-trust service.
- Decide whether a paid diagnostic, proposal, preview, minimum engagement, qualification step, or pricing conversation fits the specific business.

## NEVER DO

- Never claim a narrow focus, distinctive method, result, credential, case-study outcome, exclusivity, or premium position that evidence cannot support.
- Never treat paid discovery, no-free-work, a minimum engagement, selective applications, deposits, fixed pricing, or any Win Without Pitching practice as a universal sales policy.
- Never use “authority” to hide weak value, avoid clear scope, pressure a buyer, refuse legitimate clarification, or make a false scarcity claim.
- Never turn a client brief into an unapproved public claim, offer, contract, price, or external outreach.
- Never replace a user’s explicit collaboration, preview, accessibility, access, or commercial constraint with a rigid posture doctrine.

## GROUND THE POSITIONING DECISION

1. Read applicable `AGENTS.md` files and relevant active project truth under `.agents/contexts/` when it exists. Separate approved facts, client statements, observations, hypotheses, and unknowns.
2. Identify the service reality: current capability, repeatable method, capacity, delivery constraints, pricing model, evidence, buyer risk, and desired commercial relationship.
3. Identify the buyer decision: who is involved, what problem/job matters, what alternatives they compare, what they must believe, and what an honest next step costs them.
4. Load [Meaning and Evidence Foundation](../../reference/meaning-and-evidence-foundation.md) when audience, alternatives, promise, proof, or claim status is materially uncertain. Load [Customer, Market, and Demand Evidence](../../reference/customer-market-demand-evidence.md) only when a material market, offer, or demand decision needs evidence.
5. Select the commercial posture with the user; do not infer it from a premium aesthetic, a service category, or a book.

## SELECT A COMMERCIAL POSTURE

| Posture | Appropriate when | Useful boundaries | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selective expert | The practice has proven focus, limited capacity, a differentiated method, and a buyer who benefits from a diagnosis-first engagement. | Fit criteria, scoped paid diagnostic, clear minimum, evidence-led recommendation. | The practice is early, the buyer needs a low-risk first step, or the evidence does not justify exclusivity. |
| Collaborative service | Discovery must be mutual, scope is still emerging, or transparency and co-design are central to delivery. | Explicit proposal scope, paid/unpaid boundary agreed with the user, clear decision responsibilities. | Collaboration is being used to give away unbounded strategy or conceal a vague offer. |
| Productised service | The work is repeatable and the buyer needs clear scope, price, time, and deliverables. | Published inclusions/exclusions, fixed or transparent tiers, simple qualification. | The work is genuinely bespoke and a false package would mislead. |
| Early credibility | The practice is building proof or entering a new market. | Honest samples, limited pilots, transparent conditions, learnable offers. | A false “application only” or premium posture is substituting for evidence. |

Use the smallest posture that protects both parties and fits the actual service. Posture is a commercial choice, not a moral hierarchy.

## DIAGNOSE THE OFFER AND SURFACE

Inspect only the relevant layers; a quick review may need only claim, proof, and inquiry language.

| Layer | Question | Useful correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus and alternatives | Does the buyer understand the particular problem, context, or audience served, and why they would not simply use an alternative? | Narrow an over-broad claim only when the service and evidence support it. |
| Expertise claim | Is the promised change specific, credible, and within delivery control? | State who, problem, method, and limited value without inventing an outcome. |
| Proof architecture | What evidence makes the claim reasonable? | Use authentic work, process, scope-limited case evidence, credentials, samples, and decisions—not generic logos or adjectives. |
| Mutual fit | Can both sides decide whether the work is a fit? | Replace an auditioning posture with clear questions, scope, constraints, and a respectful decision path. |
| Diagnosis and prescription | Is the buyer’s requested solution proven, or does the work require discovery? | Offer discovery proportionate to uncertainty; explain what is known, assumed, and still to decide. |
| Commercial boundaries | What is included before engagement, what is paid, and what is contingent on contract or deposit? | Set a truthful, user-approved scope boundary; a short preview or proposal may be appropriate. |
| Price and engagement | What investment, pricing form, and payment sequence fit the service and buyer? | Surface constraints early enough to avoid wasted work; do not invent a floor or force fixed pricing. |
| Offer architecture | Is the service an unstructured menu or a comprehensible path? | Define phases, outputs, decision rights, inclusions, exclusions, and handoffs. |
| Copy posture | Does the language communicate competence without pleading, hype, or contempt? | Use precise, peer-professional language; "I’d love to help" is not automatically a defect. |
| Portfolio authority | Does work show judgement, constraints, process, and scoped outcomes? | Build case studies around situation, decision, application, evidence, and limit. |
| Inquiry flow | Does the next step gather the minimum information needed for a fit decision? | Use proportional questions and a clear response expectation; do not create an obstructive gate. |

## BUILD AN HONEST POSITION

1. State the audience and decision context without creating a demographic stereotype or fictional buyer.
2. Compare the service with real alternatives, including doing nothing or using an internal/manual process when relevant.
3. Form a differentiated promise that the actual method and evidence can carry. Prefer an explicit limit or non-fit over inflated certainty.
4. Choose proof that matches the claim. Attribute client statements and scope case-study outcomes to their conditions.
5. Specify the desired next action, mutual-fit question, commitment, and what happens next. Do not manufacture urgency or restricted access.
6. Keep discovery, proposal, execution, and agreement boundaries visible. A paid diagnostic is a viable option only when the user chooses it and its value is clear.

## OUTPUT SHAPE

For a bounded audit:

1. **Current signal:** what is clear, generic, unsupported, or mismatched.
2. **Evidence and limits:** facts used, assumptions, missing proof, and claim risks.
3. **Recommended posture:** selected option and why it fits this business; state reasonable alternatives where the choice is not settled.
4. **Smallest improvements:** copy, proof, offer, portfolio, or inquiry changes within authority.
5. **Handoff or approval:** only where the next step exceeds the requested scope.

For a full positioning decision, add an audience/alternative/promise/proof/non-fit/next-action record. Use qualitative layer statuses or a user-approved rubric; do not manufacture a precise authority score.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE CHECKLIST

1. Tie focus, promise, proof, and commercial posture to actual service reality.
2. Treat selectivity and paid discovery as options, not proof of superiority.
3. Preserve buyer dignity, clarity, access, and informed choice.
4. Flag unsupported claims and material unknowns rather than polishing them into certainty.
5. Keep proposals, pricing, contracts, publication, and outreach within explicit authority.
