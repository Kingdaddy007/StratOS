# Professional Services Posture

Use this reference when the task needs a full positioning audit, a discovery/proposal boundary, a service model, a portfolio story, or an inquiry path. Select the posture that matches service reality; do not treat any model as a default signal of quality.

## Select a posture deliberately

| Option | What it optimises | Preconditions | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selective expert | Focus, scarce capacity, diagnosis-first work, and high buyer risk. | Credible method, proof, capacity constraint, and a clear paid diagnostic value. | Performing exclusivity without evidence or using qualification to avoid clarity. |
| Collaborative service | Mutual discovery and transparent shaping of bespoke work. | Shared decision responsibilities and a bounded proposal/discovery process. | Unpaid, unlimited strategy work or vague scope. |
| Productised service | Simplicity, repeatability, speed, and comparability. | Stable deliverables, known constraints, and predictable delivery. | Hiding bespoke risk inside a fake fixed package. |
| Early credibility | Learning, pilot evidence, and honest entry into a market. | Transparent pilot terms, realistic capacity, and consent to learn. | Pretending that limited proof is established authority. |

## Diagnose proportional layers

For each required layer, record: current signal, supporting evidence, confidence/limit, and the smallest correction.

1. **Focus and substitute reduction.** Identify whether the service is understood as a particular answer to a costly, recurring problem or merely as a generic supplier. Include real alternatives and the status quo.
2. **Expertise claim.** State audience, context, problem, method, and bounded value. A useful form is: "For [audience] facing [problem], we use [method] to help them [credible change]." It is a drafting aid, not a promise formula.
3. **Proof architecture.** Match proof to the claim: work/process for method, authenticated case evidence for outcomes, credentials for qualifications, and attributed customer language for experience. Keep scope and dates.
4. **Mutual-fit process.** Explain what each side needs to learn. Replace pitch theatre with a conversation only if the buyer still gets clear, usable information.
5. **Diagnosis and prescription.** Where the request is a self-diagnosis rather than an established need, separate initial evidence gathering from a recommendation. A preliminary conversation may still be appropriate.
6. **Commercial boundary.** State what can be shared before engagement, what work is paid, what requires a contract, and who decides. Do not assume every strategy fragment must be paid or every proposal must be free.
7. **Price and engagement.** Select pricing and payment terms for cost structure, buyer risk, market, capacity, legal/contract constraints, and the service model. A floor or deposit is meaningful only when the business actually uses it.
8. **Offer architecture.** Make phases, decisions, outputs, dependencies, exclusions, and completion criteria understandable. Use a diagnostic phase only when it resolves a real uncertainty.
9. **Copy posture.** Replace vague, pleading, or inflated language with respectful precision. Avoid using superiority, hostility, or artificial formality as a proxy for confidence.
10. **Portfolio authority.** Show situation, constraints, decision, method, application, evidence, and outcome limits. A visual gallery can still be useful when visual evidence is central.
11. **Inquiry flow.** Ask only questions necessary to determine fit, scope, timing, constraints, and response path. Explain what happens after submission and preserve an accessible alternative.

## Optional commercial patterns

### Paid diagnostic

Use when a defined investigation produces independent value, the buyer understands the output, and the work reduces uncertainty before a larger engagement. Define purpose, inputs, method, output, time, fee, ownership, and what it does not guarantee. Do not rename an ordinary sales call as a paid diagnostic merely to create pressure.

### Minimum engagement

Use when capacity and delivery economics require a clear lower bound. State the basis plainly and provide an honest alternative, smaller service, or no-fit response where possible. Do not invent a minimum to signal premium status.

### Proposal or preview

Use a bounded preview, outline, sample, or proposal when it helps the buyer make an informed decision and the user authorises it. Protect proprietary or expensive strategic work through clear scope, not a blanket prohibition on showing competence.

## Language calibration

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Generic capability: "we do everything for everyone" | Specific but truthful focus and service scope. |
| Unsupported superiority: "the leading experts" | Evidence-led credential, method, or clearly attributed result. |
| Defensive gate: "apply only if you qualify" | Plain fit criteria and a respectful next step. |
| Vague submission CTA: "contact us" | A meaningful action with the expected commitment and response path. |
| Pressure: "act now before spaces disappear" | A genuine deadline or capacity limit, if one exists and is relevant. |
