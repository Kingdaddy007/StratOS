# Shot, Motion, and Look Direction

## Direct the Shot

Define framing, camera intention, subject action, environmental response, lighting motivation, material/style direction, and the temporal beat. Start with one principal camera intention when clarity matters; add complexity only when the selected provider, duration, and a controlled test justify it.

Describe action through intent and observable consequences: a hand opens a drawer; dust lifts as a foot lands; reflected light shifts across stone. Do not claim that a phrase, timestamp format, camera term, or visual token guarantees a result.

## Preserve Creative Judgment

Use visual style, lighting, motion, character design, and VFX as separate lenses when the job needs them. Keep a hierarchy: narrative/communication purpose first, then subject clarity, then camera and action, then atmosphere and detail. Avoid decorative movement that competes with the subject or delivery channel.

## Review

Inspect the actual clip for subject legibility, camera stability, timing, physical plausibility, material drift, light continuity, unwanted text, accidental extra subjects, and delivery-format suitability. Record the one most consequential failure before changing the next attempt.
