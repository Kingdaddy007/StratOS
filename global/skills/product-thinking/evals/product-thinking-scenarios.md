# Product Thinking Behaviour Scenarios

These scenarios test decisions and restraint. They do not test whether an answer repeats a named framework.

## Scoring

For each scenario, assess whether the response identifies the decision, scales effort to consequence and reversibility, identifies the decision-changing uncertainty, names an evidence limit, and selects a credible next action. Penalise unnecessary ceremony, AI-by-default, metrics-by-default, and treating any one signal as universal truth.

## Positive scenarios

### 1. Unspecified internal AI assistant

An operations leader asks for an AI assistant to handle internal approvals. No current workflow, approvers, error cost, or override process has been described.

**Expected:** frame the actor, workflow, current alternative, authority, error cost, and whether AI adds value; recommend a narrow observation or manual pilot before architecture.

### 2. High-upside greenfield idea

A founder has enthusiasm and hypothetical interest, but no evidence of a repeated problem or existing alternatives.

**Expected:** treat enthusiasm as a hypothesis; identify actor, context, alternative, adoption conditions, and the smallest credible problem/value test with a learning rule.

### 3. Private tool instrumentation request

A one-person internal tool is used weekly. Its owner requests a comprehensive analytics dashboard.

**Expected:** ask what decision analytics must inform and compare a manual log, observation, or short debrief; do not impose event taxonomies if a lighter signal is enough.

### 4. Difficult-to-reverse sensitive workflow change

A public-facing workflow will process sensitive personal data and cannot easily be rolled back.

**Expected:** choose high-stakes treatment; identify parties, privacy/security needs, failure boundaries, representative evaluation, human control, staged exposure, fallback, and approval gates.

### 5. Similar opportunity scores

Two opportunities have close RICE scores; one creates vendor lock-in and the other is reversible but has lower apparent reach.

**Expected:** expose score assumptions and compare reversibility, dependencies, cost of delay, and evidence confidence; recommend a discriminating next action rather than choosing arithmetic alone.

## Restraint scenarios

### 6. Approved website copy

A user supplies exact approved copy for a low-risk marketing page and asks for the replacement.

**Expected:** make the scoped change and perform a basic acceptance check; do not create personas, discovery, an MVP, or analytics work.

### 7. Small private prototype adjustment

A developer provides an exact acceptance example for a reversible layout adjustment.

**Expected:** use the acceptance example and state an assumption only if material; do not demand a research phase.

### 8. Deterministic task proposed as AI

A team wants AI for a narrow rules-based validation task where the rule is known, transparent, cheap, and easy to maintain.

**Expected:** compare the deterministic baseline and recommend it unless AI creates demonstrated unique value.
