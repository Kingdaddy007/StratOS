# Speculative Outreach

## WHEN TO USE THIS

- Build or present a tailored proof-of-concept before speaking with a prospect.
- Convert public research into a respectful problem hypothesis and low-friction outreach message.
- Diagnose and improve the sent-to-viewed-to-replied-to-booked outreach sequence before scaling.

## NEVER DO

- Invent confidential problems, customer complaints, revenue loss, conversion data, or business urgency.
- Present an inference as a fact or make the prospect correct an aggressive diagnosis.
- Ask for extensive discovery information before demonstrating relevant value.
- Describe an incomplete concept as a finished website, strategy, or guaranteed result.
- Use animation, personalization, or praise that does not prove the outreach thesis.
- Publish, deploy, or publicly reuse prospect assets without permission.
- Scale or automate an approach before manual evidence shows that it earns useful responses.

## STATUS AND OPERATING MODEL

Treat this method as provisional. Preserve its decision logic while improving language, sequence, and thresholds from real outreach evidence.

Use this chain:

`research -> observed gap -> respectful hypothesis -> build-first proof -> concise presentation -> binary next step -> measure -> iterate -> scale`

Make the prospect's first decision small: whether the demonstrated direction is relevant enough to discuss. Do not ask them to approve a full engagement from a first message.

## EVIDENCE AND ASSUMPTION LADDER

Separate every material statement:

- **FACT:** Publicly observable and attributable.
- **REPORTED:** Stated by the prospect or another source but not independently demonstrated.
- **INFERENCE:** A plausible interpretation of the facts.
- **UNKNOWN:** Material information unavailable before discovery.

Build the assumed problem in four steps:

1. State the observable condition without judgment.
2. Identify the perception, workflow, or experience gap it may create.
3. Describe the possible business consequence as a hypothesis, not a fact.
4. Build a demonstration that makes the proposed improvement visible.

Prefer: `This may be leaving the value difficult to perceive before inquiry.`

Reject: `You are losing customers and money because your website is bad.`

Assume only what public evidence can reasonably support. Keep deeper operational, financial, customer, team, and strategic claims for post-interest discovery.

## PROSPECT THESIS

Define these before making the concept:

1. **Observed condition:** What is visibly true?
2. **Perception gap:** What might the current presentation make difficult to understand, trust, remember, or value?
3. **Desired revaluation:** What should the prospect or their customer perceive differently?
4. **Demonstration:** What small artifact can prove that possibility?
5. **Honest limit:** What does the artifact intentionally leave unresolved?
6. **Binary next step:** What simple response advances the conversation?

State the thesis in one sentence:

`I observed [fact], hypothesized [gap], and built [artifact] to demonstrate [possible improvement].`

Reject the thesis if the artifact could be sent unchanged to another prospect.

## BUILD-FIRST PROOF

Build the smallest convincing artifact that demonstrates judgment before requesting discovery. Use a prototype, teardown, workflow, mockup, calculator, sample, or short strategic reconstruction appropriate to the prospect.

Require every material feature or visual move to prove the assumed problem or desired revaluation. Remove spectacle that only advertises the creator's technical ability.

For speculative websites:

- Use the prospect's real public identity and strongest available assets.
- Keep the prototype private and clearly label its incomplete scope.
- Present depth honestly; do not manufacture portfolio scale, testimonials, services, results, or project relationships.
- Let the experience prove the pitch. Keep meta-commentary about the prospect's current weakness in the outreach narration, not in the customer-facing concept.
- Treat cinematic motion as semantic choreography: it must change understanding, organize proof, reveal a relationship, or create a justified perception shift.

## MESSAGE ARCHITECTURE

Write for a stranger with little attention and no obligation to respond.

1. **Specific observation:** Show enough research to establish relevance.
2. **Gentle hypothesis:** Name the opportunity without insulting the current work.
3. **Built proof:** Say what was made and why.
4. **Scope limit:** Clarify that it is a demonstration, not a completed strategy or demand.
5. **Easy access:** Place the preview or walkthrough directly in reach.
6. **Binary invitation:** Ask whether the direction feels relevant enough to discuss.

Keep the message human, compact, and easy to scan. Prefer concrete language and short sentences. Remove ceremonial compliments, long explanations, generic AI phrasing, stacked adjectives, and repeated reassurance.

Do not force a fixed script across prospects. Rebuild the observation and hypothesis from each lead's evidence. Reuse the structure, not the personality or wording.

## WALKTHROUGH LOGIC

Open the recorded walkthrough with the thought behind the artifact, then show the artifact quickly.

- Explain the observed condition in one or two sentences.
- State the hypothesis as an opportunity, not an accusation.
- Explain what the demonstration is testing.
- Begin the visual proof before attention decays.
- Narrate decisions only when the explanation increases perceived relevance.
- End with the smallest honest next step.

Personalize the first seconds and the demonstrated evidence. Do not hide a generic presentation behind a customized greeting.

## FUNNEL DIAGNOSIS AND ITERATION

Track at minimum:

`sent -> delivered -> viewed -> replied -> positive reply -> meeting booked -> showed -> progressed or closed`

Diagnose the earliest weak transition:

| Bottleneck | Inspect First | Improve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sent to viewed | Sender identity, subject, teaser, thumbnail, delivery | Packaging and relevance signal |
| Viewed to replied | Research quality, assumed problem, proof, message length | Personalization, clarity, offer, friction |
| Replied to booked | Response speed, next-step burden, scheduling | Simplicity and momentum |
| Booked to showed | Qualification, reminders, perceived value | Follow-up and expectation setting |
| Showed to progressed | Discovery, fit, proof, offer, objection handling | Sales conversation and offer fit |

Validate effectiveness manually before optimizing scalability. Change one meaningful variable at a time when sample size allows. Preserve qualitative replies; they explain why a numerical stage moved.

## ANTI-PATTERNS

| Anti-Pattern | What It Is | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery Before Value | Asking a stranger for detailed private information | Research publicly and demonstrate a bounded hypothesis first |
| Fake Diagnosis | Declaring financial or identity damage without proof | Use observable facts and conditional consequences |
| Generic Customization | Swapping a name or logo into a reusable demo | Tie the thesis, evidence, and artifact to the specific prospect |
| Portfolio Theater | Implying more work, scale, or results than exist | Curate fewer assets with honest depth and labels |
| Cinematic Wallpaper | Using motion only to create a wow reaction | Give motion a communication job and static meaning |
| AI Voice | Long, over-fitted, ceremonial, repetitive prose | Shorten, humanize, and retain only decision-relevant meaning |
| Premature Scaling | Automating before the offer earns replies | Validate manually, diagnose the funnel, then systematize |

## OUTPUT SHAPE

For a new prospect, return:

1. **Evidence snapshot:** Facts, inferences, unknowns, and source limits.
2. **Assumed problem:** Observation, perception gap, possible consequence, and confidence.
3. **Build thesis:** Artifact, desired revaluation, proof job, and honest limit.
4. **Outreach message:** One recommended concise draft.
5. **Walkthrough opening:** Short narration that reaches the proof quickly.
6. **Binary next step:** One low-friction question.
7. **Claim cautions:** Wording that needs validation or softer framing.

For an outreach review, return the earliest funnel bottleneck, evidence, highest-leverage change, test, and measurement plan.

## NON-NEGOTIABLE CHECKLIST

1. Ground the observation in prospect-specific public evidence.
2. Label facts, inferences, possible consequences, and unknowns correctly.
3. Make the artifact prove the outreach thesis.
4. State incomplete scope honestly.
5. Keep the message concise, human, and prospect-specific.
6. Reduce the first response to a small yes-or-no relevance decision.
7. Avoid revenue, conversion, prestige, and outcome guarantees.
8. Keep prospect assets private until permission changes the boundary.
9. Validate effectiveness before scaling or automation.
10. Treat the method and wording as iterative, not fixed doctrine.
