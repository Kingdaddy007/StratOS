# Meaning and Evidence Foundation

**Type:** conditional shared reference  
**Status:** active  
**Use with:** brand, positioning, narrative, drafting, editing, or behavioural-insight work where the meaning, claim, proof, or audience decision is materially uncertain.

## Purpose and loading rule

This reference gives related communication capabilities one compact language for making and checking decisions. It is available in the General payload for discovery, but it does **not** activate the Growth or Spatial pack, create a compulsory workshop, or replace the owning skill.

Load the smallest relevant part only when a request needs to decide or challenge:

- who the audience is and in what context they decide;
- the job, problem, desired change, category, or practical alternative;
- a differentiated promise, a reason to believe it, or an honest non-fit;
- the order in which meaning, proof, and a next action should appear; or
- whether a factual, comparative, experiential, credential, testimonial, or outcome claim can be written truthfully.

A clear local correction, grammar fix, or bounded rewrite remains direct work. Do not manufacture a strategy exercise merely because a request contains marketing language.

## Shared decision objects

Use only the objects that resolve the uncertainty. They are prompts for judgment, not a mandatory worksheet.

| Decision | Question | Typical evidence or limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience and context | Who is deciding or influencing, in what situation and channel? | Brief, observed behaviour, approved research, public context; never stereotype or invent a persona. |
| Job or desired change | What is the person trying to accomplish, avoid, or understand? | Product reality, research, support data, or client statements. |
| Alternatives and category | What do they compare this with, postpone, or do manually? | Public alternatives and customer language are inputs, not proof of demand. |
| Promise and differentiation | What credible change is offered, for whom, and why this option? | The offer must be capable of delivering it; narrower language is safer when proof is thin. |
| Reason to believe | Why should a reasonable person believe the promise? | Demonstration, process, credentials, scope-limited case evidence, authentic testimonial, or data. |
| Constraints and non-fit | What cannot be promised, who should not proceed, and under what conditions? | Product limits, capacity, price, delivery constraints, and approved policy. |
| Meaning and message order | What must be understood first, next, and last? | Audience comprehension, task, channel, available proof, and accessibility needs. |
| Next action | What truthful, informed, proportionate action is available? | Must match user intent, commitment level, access, and service capacity. |

## Evidence and claim discipline

Label material statements rather than allowing a fluent draft to turn them into facts:

- **fact** — verified by a reliable source or approved first-party record;
- **reported statement** — an attributed claim, not independently verified truth;
- **interpretation** — a reasoned reading of evidence, with limits;
- **hypothesis** — a testable possibility, including AI-generated wording or audience assumptions;
- **recommendation** — a proposed decision or action, not permission to act; and
- **unknown** — information that has not been established.

For a public claim, retain the source, relevant scope, time period, confidence, and approval state where doing so is proportionate. Customer language can inform vocabulary and comprehension; it does not prove population prevalence, actual demand, or causal conversion. A case study or A/B result must retain its conditions and cannot be promoted into a universal law.

Never invent customer language, testimonials, credentials, results, comparative superiority, scarcity, cultural insight, private motives, or proof. AI-generated content is a hypothesis or draft until an accountable person verifies its facts and approves its use.

## Three levels of effort

| Level | Use when | Minimum result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | A bounded edit or draft has a known audience, offer, and factual basis. | The requested revision, with an explicit placeholder or question only for a material unknown. |
| Deliberate | Positioning, audience, promise, proof, or message order is genuinely unclear. | A compact decision record covering only the needed objects above. |
| Evidence-gated | The work includes regulated or vulnerable audiences, a material public claim, major repositioning, synthetic/edited representation that could mislead, or another high-consequence decision. | Claim register, uncertainty, applicable handoff, and an approval stop before any external effect. |

## Ethical and authority boundary

Use communication to reduce unnecessary confusion and help an informed person decide. Do not use fake scarcity, hidden defaults, confirmshaming, obstructive cancellation, coercive urgency, deceptive comparison, stereotype-based targeting, or exploitation of vulnerable audiences.

Read-only public desk research within the task scope is allowed. Stop for explicit human approval immediately before an external effect: contacting people, collecting personal or sensitive data, publishing or submitting material, making a public claim, advertising spend, payment, CRM mutation, or any other action outside the authorised task. This reference never grants that approval.

For synthetic or materially edited media, keep truthful creation and edit information when the audience could reasonably mistake it for documentary evidence, a real person, or a real outcome. Provenance can show an association or change history; it cannot make a false claim true or a work trustworthy by itself.

## Source and review notes

This is a Studio synthesis, not a legal rulebook. It is based on the 2026-08-19 portable brand/positioning research assessment and the following primary sources, rechecked 2026-08-19:

- [Google Search guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content): user value, accuracy, quality, relevance, and context about automated creation.
- [C2PA 2.4 specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html): provenance is tamper-evident association information, not a judgment that content is good or true.
- [W3C WCAG 2 overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/): accessibility and understandability are part of communication quality; technical conformance belongs with UI/UX work.
- [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence): a voluntary lifecycle-oriented risk resource, not a requirement to create a heavyweight compliance programme.

Recheck channel, advertising, privacy, consumer-protection, and disclosure requirements for the relevant country, sector, platform, and date before external use. This reference supplies no legal conclusion.
